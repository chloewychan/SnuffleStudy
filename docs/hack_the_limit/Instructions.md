## Project Story

### About the Project

*Be sure to write what inspired you, what you learned, how you built your project, and the challenges you faced. Format your story in Markdown, with LaTeX support for math.*

### Inspiration

It's never difficult to care about how well you do in school. The most difficult challenge is staying motivated to actually study.

One of the post popular solutions is studying as a group, as this method is used by people in all industries, ages, and regions. This introduces a new problem of finding the time and location for everyone to meet together, since schedules never sync and plans are always changing.

A common fix is calling online and “coworking,” popularized after COVID-19 forced us to meet virtually instead of in-person. Here’s where the nit-picky issues arise. Studying in public locations, conserving battery, and other issues, forces people to turn their cameras and microphones off. This kills the accountability and motivating atmosphere of studying as a group. 

Our solution is SnuffleStudy. When you’re off-task, it notifies your friends, giving you a reason to stay focused. When you’re sensing your friends are off task, send them a Producer Tag to remind them you’re counting on them to lock in. And if all else fails, keep distracting sites locked and give your friends the passcode.

### What it does

SnuffleStudy has four main features to keep users “locked in”: Snuffles the bunny, study sessions, friend groups, and permission passcodes.

1. Bunny - Snuffles and the User

Snuffles is a hand-drawn bunny with a cute and aesthetic charm. This makes the SnuffleStudy and inviting and fun extension, rather than just another blocker extension. The user can choose a name for the bunny (instead of Snuffles), and a name for themselves.

Based on the user’s work-life balance, how well the user focused in previous study sessions, and their interaction with friends, status bars show Snuffles’ happiness, productivity, and friendliness.

2. Study - Study Sessions and the Task Vault

The concept of SnuffleStudy is centred around the study timer. Each session has a goal, focus duration, pressure style, and restriction mode.

- The task vault is a to-do list that can be set as the goal for a study session. This ensures the user is aware of their goals, can split it into subtasks, and completes it during a session. The checklist feature encourages organization and time management.
- Focus duration can be entered as minutes and/or hours, and over 60 minutes will be automatically converted to hours.
- Pressure style includes “Gentle Encouragement,” “Strict Coach,” “Ruthless Enforcer,” and more. These pressure styles change the tone of how Snuffles gives encouraging reminders. Although some pressure styles are more harsh than others, Snuffles ensures to make all comments constructive and work-ethic-based, not personal attacks that would deter the user.
- There are two restriction modes: “Soft - nudge & escalate” and “Hard - passcode required.” The Soft mode will give reminders to stay on track when the user is inactive or visiting restricted sites, whereas the Hard mode will completely block the user from using restricted sites unless the user gets a passcode from their friend.

3. Friends - Study Rooms, Friends, and Producer Tags

The real innovative feature is Study Rooms with friends. Users can follow friends, create/join a study room, add their friends, and study together.

- Cameras and microphones are optional. Instead of keeping microphones on and potentially distracting each other, users can send Producer Tags, a pre-recorded short audio message, that just says “hey, I'm still here, get back to it."
- If users go inactive for too long or wander onto a restricted site, your friend group gets notified. Accountability doesn’t stop just because the call is muted. Your friend is then able to nudge you, send you a Producer Tag, or message you some other way to keep you in check.

4. Settings - Passcode Requests

- Temporary requests allows users to ask their friends to unlock a specific blocked feature. For example, instead of all blocked sites becoming available after the user acquires the passcode, the user gets a temporary passcode that only unlocks one site, like youtube.com. These requests are sent through SnuffleStudy.
- Unlock requests allows users to remove all blockers at once. They can get this passcode by requesting through SnuffleStudy, or asking their friend outside of this extension and inputting their passcode in manually.

### How we built it

- **Chrome extension, Manifest V3, TypeScript + React**, built with **WXT**
- Domain / infrastructure / presentation layered architecture, not one giant popup.js
    - The extension is extremely scalable due to the structured architecture
- Local-first: `chrome.storage` + IndexedDB, so the core app works fully offline even if the backend is down
- Backend on **Supabase**
    - Postgres, Auth, Realtime, Storage, Edge Functions
    - Friend groups, live status, and nudges built with row-level security
- Study Room video calls with **LiveKit**
- AI-generated coaching messages using **Anthropic's Claude API**
    - Called from a Supabase Edge Function so the key never ships inside the extension
    - Falls back instantly to a pre-written line if the network's slow or down
- Temporary passcode emails with **Resend**
- Frontend designed in **Figma**
    - The UI’s soft handmade feel is built without AI
    - **Rough** Figma plugin is used to add texture to fills and strokes
- Images drawn in **Procreate**

### Challenges we ran into

The biggest challenge we ran into was letting an extension watch what sites you're on and tell your friends about it, since it could run into security and trust issues. Here are some ways we combatted this issue:

- We created the two tracking tiers: the first is activity-only and doesn’t ask for any site permissions, whereas the second asks for permission the moment it is turned on.
- Every piece of data a friend can see has its own toggle, off by default, to ensure no user is afraid that their information is accidentally being sent to people. This is enforced with row-level security in the database, not just hidden in the UI, so it can’t leak even if someone messes with the client.
- Another method to prevent accidental lock-outs is soft mode vs. hard mode. Soft mode nudges the user (Snuffles and friends can pressure), although you can still continue with your distractions. Hard mode locks you out completely, with no in-app escape hatch, only a passcode. This is stored as a salted hash with rate-limited guessing, never plaintext.
- There are two kinds of passcodes: a permanent one, and a temporary one. The permanent one is set once and handed to a friend (not yourself), which is the same idea as sharing a Screen Time code. The temporary, single-site one can be sent with a message and approved by a friend remotely, for the "I swear I need this one page" case. This is also stored as a salted hash with rate-limited guessing.
- No API key (Anthropic's, Supabase's, LiveKit's, Resend's) ever ships inside the extension itself. Everything goes through a server-side function that holds the real secret.

### Accomplishments that we're proud of

- Hand-designing the full UI in Figma and hand-drawing the bunny designs in Procreate to get that handmade, friendly vibe. Snuffles specifically was drawn to feel like an actual bunny to cheer you on, not a generic app mascot. Many programmers use rubber ducks to explain their thought process out loud to someone, and Snuffles mirrors this type of connection.
- Getting a real, *live* friend-accountability system — live status, nudges, remote unlock approval, daily digest — working on top of an app that still works completely offline if you never add a single friend. The social layer bolted on cleanly instead of turning into a rewrite, because of that architecture call up front

### What we learned

- MV3 service workers can get suspended at any moment — you can't trust anything sitting in memory, so every timer had to be rebuilt around real timestamps and `chrome.alarms`
- Saying a feature is "privacy-respecting" and actually enforcing it at the database layer (not just hiding it in the UI) are two very different amounts of work
- Isolating every third-party service — Supabase, LiveKit, the Claude API — behind its own small adapter made this way less stressful to build. Swapping a provider later touches one file, not the whole app

### What's next for SnuffleStudy

- A real, persistent Snuffles you can drag anywhere on your screen and click to open your side panel (right now he's just a placeholder image that shows up during warnings)
- An actual analytics dashboard on top of the session history we already track
- Push notifications, so nudges arrive instantly instead of on a polling interval
- More Snuffles personalities/pressure styles, and a bigger Play Mode — mini-games, cosmetics
- Multi-device sync, and eventually getting off Chrome-only
- Actually publishing it: privacy policy, account deletion, a real Chrome Web Store listing
